The San Francisco 49ers are releasing wide receiver Brandon Aiyuk, ESPN's Adam Schefter reported Monday. The move comes as the two-week roster exemption the team received when Aiyuk returned was set to run out.

For 49ers fans, it closes a long and messy chapter with a former first-round pick. Aiyuk hasn't played in an NFL game since October 2024, and no trade ever came together.

The team put Aiyuk on the reserve/left squad list in December 2025, and he stayed there until November 2026. He then hired agent Drew Rosenhaus, met with the 49ers on Nov. 6 and was reinstated on Nov. 9. That reinstatement started the exemption clock.

The team had signaled for months that it was in no hurry. Head coach Kyle Shanahan admitted at the end of March that there was no rush. After April's draft, general manager John Lynch said he had no plans to release Aiyuk "anytime soon" and invited interested teams to call. In January, Lynch said it was "safe to say" Aiyuk had played his last snap as a Niner, adding that the receiver had stopped coming to the facility or communicating with the team.

Things got stranger over the summer. On June 3, it became public that the Santa Clara County District Attorney's office had issued an arrest warrant for Aiyuk, alleging misdemeanor exhibition of speed on or about Dec. 20, 2025. That stemmed from a YouTube video of him driving over 100 mph near Levi's Stadium. Aiyuk later aimed insults at the team in Instagram videos, and many of his posts seemed to push for a move to the Commanders.

## A big talent with a complicated exit

Aiyuk was drafted by San Francisco in 2020 and broke out in 2022 and 2023, catching 153 passes for 2,357 yards and 15 touchdowns. He held in during most of the 2024 preseason while seeking a new deal. The Steelers had a trade in place before he agreed to a four-year extension with the 49ers.

He tore his ACL and MCL seven games into that new deal and never played for the team again. For his career, he has 294 catches for 4,305 yards and 25 touchdowns in 69 games.

The way he became available could make some teams cautious, and clubs will have to weigh the good against the bad.
