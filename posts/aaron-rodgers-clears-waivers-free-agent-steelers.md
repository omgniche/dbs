Aaron Rodgers cleared waivers Thursday and is now a free agent, NFL Network Insider Ian Rapoport reported. The Steelers released the 42-year-old quarterback a day earlier.

For Steelers fans, this closes a chaotic few days for a 6-4 team. Rodgers was benched in favor of Mason Rudolph on Tuesday night, then went on The Pat McAfee Show and went after head coach Mike McCarthy.

Rodgers claimed McCarthy has dozed off in meetings and skipped other meetings and team events. He said he had raised the coach's inattentiveness with him and had talked about McCarthy giving up play-calling duties. "If you try and bench me or don't change the play-calling, then you should just release me, because I feel like, at this point, I'm doing what's right for the organization," Rodgers said.

## Steelers respond

McCarthy spoke to reporters hours after the McAfee appearance. Without giving details, he said Rodgers had issued an "ultimatum" that was "outside of his job description," and that benching the quarterback was his call.

On Thursday, team president Art Rooney II sided with McCarthy in an interview with The Pittsburgh Post-Gazette. He criticized Rodgers for "insubordination" and for not being a "good teammate."

Rodgers signed a one-year deal this past spring that included $22 million guaranteed to return to Pittsburgh for a second season. He said the decision was partly because the team hired McCarthy after Mike Tomlin's 19-year run ended.

Rodgers and McCarthy worked together for 13 years in Green Bay, where they beat Rooney's Steelers in Super Bowl XLV.

Pittsburgh hosts the Denver Broncos (8-2) on Friday at 3 p.m. ET.
