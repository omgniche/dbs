Anthony Edwards doesn't know when he'll be back on the floor for the Minnesota Timberwolves. Speaking Thursday for the first time since his Oct. 29 knee surgery, the guard said his right knee will make that call, according to the Associated Press.

"I have no clue. Whenever my knee tells me it's ready, then I'll be ready," Edwards said.

The only loose expectation is that he returns at some point this regular season. Until then, Edwards is doing rehab every day, with two sessions on game days. He's also staying involved with the team, sending scouting reports and encouragement to teammates in the group chat.

## How it unfolded

Edwards played through right knee pain all of last season and appeared in a career-low 61 games. He felt fine after summer workouts and training camp, but the pain returned in the second game of this season, at Orlando on Oct. 23. He had heavy swelling before the home opener against Toronto two days later.

He then scored 47 points in an overtime loss at Cleveland on Oct. 26, bringing his total to a franchise-record 133 points through four games. An MRI the next day found new damage that earlier scans had missed. "I'm the type of guy who never wanted to have surgery," Edwards said. "I thought it would just go away."

## Why it matters

This is the longest Edwards has ever been sidelined, and he is not a player who likes to sit. The Wolves have helped ease the wait by winning seven of their first eight games this month with LaMelo Ball added to the lineup. Ball's arrival also moved Edwards back to shooting guard.

"I'm thirsty to come back, but not super fast because of how they're playing. I don't want to take them out of rhythm," Edwards said.

Minnesota is also still without Donte DiVincenzo, who tore his right Achilles tendon in the same playoff game against Denver in which Edwards hyperextended his left knee. DiVincenzo has been cleared for on-court shooting drills, while Edwards is still rebuilding strength in his leg.

Edwards, 25, hopes the surgery means years of playing without pain. "By the grace of God, if I can slide my feet, jump around a bit, I'll be out there," he said.
