Astros general manager Randy Flores said Tuesday he hopes to decide on the team's next manager soon, possibly by the end of this week. He spoke at the General Managers Meetings in Las Vegas, his first time addressing the media since being hired nearly two weeks ago.

The job opened when Houston chose not to renew Joe Espada's contract after three seasons. For fans, the choice shapes everything that follows: the coaching staff, the roster plan and the team's direction this offseason.

Four known candidates have interviewed: former Astros catcher Brad Ausmus, Albert Pujols, former Astros third baseman Morgan Ensberg and Rangers bench coach Luis Urueta. Flores said owner Jim Crane, senior advisor Jeff Bagwell and assistant GMs Charles Cook and Gavin Dickey all took part in the process. "My hope is I get back to Houston at the end of this week, hopefully finalize our manager decision and spend the next week in bunker-down mode," Flores said.

## A staff and a roster to fill

The new manager will inherit several holes on the coaching staff. Houston needs a bench coach, plus replacements for Tim Cossins, Tommy Kawamura, Tony Perezchica, Anthony Iapoce and Ethan Katz. Field coordinator Jason Bell has left for the Red Sox, and Omar López remains in the organization in a role yet to be determined.

Flores said the staff work is "at the whiteboard stage." The front office also needs an assistant GM after Connor Huff resigned to take a job outside baseball.

On the roster, shortstop Jeremy Peña enters the final year of his contract. The Astros also need outfield bats and more pitching depth in the rotation and bullpen, and they have a surplus of infielders. Flores met with seven agent groups Tuesday, but said the in-depth talks will come once a manager is in place.

Flores has reached out to everyone on the 40-man roster. Yordan Alvarez, who is up for several awards at the MLB Awards Show, answered his text right away. "Yo Flo, let's go!" was the reply, Flores said. He also met Carlos Correa at Daikin Park last week.
