The Houston Astros have named Brad Ausmus their next manager, the team announced Wednesday. The former Astros catcher spent the last three seasons as the Yankees' bench coach and will be introduced at a press conference at Daikin Park on Friday.

For Astros fans, this is a homecoming. Ausmus caught more games than anyone else in franchise history, and he was part of the 2005 team that reached the World Series. He replaces Joe Espada, whose contract was not renewed after three seasons that included two division titles.

General manager Randy Flores, whom Houston hired two weeks ago to replace Dana Brown, said in a statement: "We are thrilled to welcome Brad back to the Astros organization." Ausmus said in his own statement: "I am very excited to be back in Houston."

## The résumé

Ausmus, 57, has managed before. He went a combined 386-422 with the Tigers (2014-17) and the Angels (2019). He won the AL Central with Detroit in 2014 and won 86 games there in 2016. He also managed Team Israel in the 2026 World Baseball Classic, where it went 2-2 and failed to advance from pool play.

He is close with Jeff Bagwell, a senior advisor to ownership and baseball operations, who took part in the hiring process. The two were teammates in Houston from 1997-98 and 2001-05.

## A famous homer

Ausmus hit one of the biggest home runs in team history, at the time: a two-out, game-tying shot off Kyle Farnsworth in the bottom of the ninth in Game 4 of the 2005 NLDS against the Braves. Houston won 7-6 on Chris Burke's walk-off homer in the 18th inning.

"As far as personal moments go, it's probably the highlight," Ausmus said of the home run. He added that going to the World Series "overall was more fun."

## What's next

The Astros now need to build a coaching staff, including a new bench coach to replace Omar López, who remains with the organization. The club did not renew catching instructor Tim Cossins, game plan coach Tommy Kawamura, third base coach Tony Perezchica, assistant hitting coach Anthony Iapoce or assistant pitching coach Ethan Katz.

Houston said last month it was keeping pitching coach Josh Miller, first-base coach Dave Clark, hitting coach Victor Rodriguez and bullpen catcher Javier Bracamonte. Field coordinator Jason Bell was also retained but later left for a job with the Red Sox.
