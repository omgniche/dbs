Bills general manager Brandon Beane walked through the Amani Hooker trade Tuesday. Buffalo sent a 2027 second-round pick to the Titans for the safety and also received Tennessee's fifth-round pick.

For Bills fans, this is a big swing at the deadline. Hooker leads the NFL in interceptions, with five in seven games this season, including three against Cincinnati in Week 8. He joins a 7-1 team whose defense has allowed just 16 total points in two games since the Week 7 bye.

Beane said he came into deadline week hunting for a skillset, not a name or position. He wanted "a true difference maker for our team," and said Buffalo was willing to pay "significant compensation." Hooker is 28, has two years left on his contract after 2026 and fits under the Bills' cap. To make the money work, Buffalo restructured Dion Dawkins' contract, and Beane thanked Dawkins and his representatives.

## How the deal came together

Beane said he first talked with the Titans about a week ago, a few days after Tennessee traded Jeffrey Simmons to the Bears. Tennessee was on its bye and wasn't ready to do anything in those early talks. Beane also crossed paths with Titans GM Mike Borgonzi at Saturday's Miami-Notre Dame game, though he said they barely discussed it.

Things stayed quiet until Tuesday. "If you asked me at noon, I didn't feel great that we were going to be able to do anything," Beane said. The talks picked up after 1 p.m. and got the deal done.

## Fit in Buffalo

Beane compared Hooker to former Bills safety Micah Hyde, noting both played at Iowa. "He's a player that has instincts, ball skills," Beane said. Hooker has 17 career interceptions and 468 tackles in his eighth season, all with Tennessee until now.

He will team with Cole Bishop. "This is a great marriage," Beane said, adding that Hooker can handle the deep post while Bishop has the versatility to do both jobs in Jim Leonhard's defense.

Whether Hooker plays this week is unclear. Beane said he is "football smart" but "going to have a lot of catching up to do," and that it depends on how quickly he gets up to speed.
