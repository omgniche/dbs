The Blue Jays have agreed to a five-year, $80 million contract with Japanese pitcher Kaima Taira, a source told MLB.com's Mark Feinsand on Tuesday. The deal includes an opt-out after the third year. The club has not confirmed it.

For Toronto fans, this is a big get after a run of near misses. The Blue Jays lost out on Tarik Skubal, and two other potential targets, Freddy Peralta and Trevor Rogers, signed five-year deals with the Orioles and Cubs. Now they have a high-upside arm to put at the top of the rotation.

Taira will play his rookie MLB season at age 27. According to MLB.com, he led NPB in ERA with a 1.36 mark over 23 games, striking out 24.8% of the batters he faced and walking 7.9%. He debuted in NPB in 2019 and has a 1.78 career ERA over eight seasons.

That starter's workload followed a couple of years in the bullpen. In one of those relief seasons, he racked up 31 saves with a 1.71 ERA.

Taira is only 5 feet 8, but he can run his four-seam fastball up into the mid-to-upper 90s. His low release point can help the pitch play up, MLB.com noted, much like Yoshinobu Yamamoto and Shota Imanaga, who are both listed at 5 feet 10. He also throws a cutter, sweeper, changeup, splitter, sinker, slider and curveball.

A Pacific Rim scout told MLB.com's Michael Clair that Taira has a "big fastball, a lot of secondaries."

Scouts weren't unanimous on his role. Some rated his stuff second-best on the open market behind only Skubal, a two-time Cy Young winner. Others thought he fit better in relief, to keep him healthy and let his fastball stay at its best.

MLB.com also pointed out that since Fred Norman in 1979, only Marcus Stroman has topped 100 innings in an MLB season at 5-8 or shorter.
