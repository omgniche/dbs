The Denver Broncos backed out of their trade with the Los Angeles Chargers for inside linebacker Daiyan Henley, the Associated Press reported. The deal was agreed Monday, a day before the trade deadline. Denver was to send the Chargers a 2027 fourth-round pick and a 2028 fifth-rounder.

The cancellation has stirred discussion over whether the NFL should change its rule on teams acquiring injured players. Chargers general manager Joe Hortiz expects teams to propose closing what he sees as a loophole at the league meetings in March.

The two sides tell different stories. A person with knowledge of the situation told the AP the Broncos were surprised by something in Henley's physical that the Chargers hadn't mentioned. After consulting medical personnel, Denver told the league and the Chargers it wanted to discuss new compensation terms, which is standard in such cases. The teams swapped proposals but couldn't agree.

Hortiz said the Broncos knew Henley was dealing with an injury when they agreed to the deal. He said Denver then tried to change the terms and used the failed physical as its reason to back out. "It's a really unfortunate thing," Hortiz said.

Chargers coach Jim Harbaugh said the Broncos used a loophole that "continues to set a bad precedent."

## What we know about Henley

The Chargers did not disclose Henley's injury. He was listed with a knee issue as recently as Week 6.

The fourth-year player has 51 tackles, two fumble recoveries, a forced fumble and an interception in eight games this season. A third-round pick out of Washington State in 2023, he became a starter in his second year. Over his last 41 games, he has 301 tackles, five sacks, four interceptions, two fumble recoveries and 15 passes defensed.

In a statement, the Broncos said, "We have great respect for Daiyan as a player and person, and we wish him all the best moving forward." Denver is 7-2 and on a bye this week. The winless Chargers (0-8) play at Baltimore on Monday night.

The Chargers also poked at the situation online. They posted a graphic reading "Welcome back, Daiyan," with the earlier "Thank you" message crossed out. Hortiz said he met with Henley and apologized for what the player went through. "It's a challenging thing, but the reality of it is we love him," Hortiz said.
