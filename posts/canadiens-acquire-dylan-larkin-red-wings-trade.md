The Montreal Canadiens acquired forward Dylan Larkin from the Detroit Red Wings in a multiplayer trade Sunday, and he is set to make his debut Monday night against the Los Angeles Kings, NHL.com reported. Puck drop is 7:30 p.m. ET.

On Oct. 10, the crowd at Bell Centre chanted "We want Larkin!" whenever the 30-year-old stepped on the ice during a 1-0 Red Wings loss in Montreal. He skated with his new teammates Monday with No. 89 on his helmet.

The Canadiens are happy about it, too. Defenseman Kaiden Guhle said the chants were unlike anything he had seen in a game, and he expects a big reaction. "I'm sure it'll be pretty loud for him out there tonight," Guhle said.

## What Montreal gave up

The Canadiens sent Kirby Dach, goaltender Sam Montembeault and prospect Michael Hage to Detroit. They also sent conditional first-round picks in 2027 and 2029, a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fourth-round pick. In addition to Larkin, Montreal received a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 third-round pick from Detroit.

Guhle said Dach is a close friend, so it was tough to see him go. "But it's part of the business and we got a pretty good player in return," he said.

## How it got here

Larkin first asked for a trade in the offseason, and the request became public. Detroit announced Sept. 11 that he was no longer captain, a role he had held since Jan. 13, 2021. On Oct. 29, interim GM Shawn Horcoff said Larkin told the team he would no longer play for the Red Wings, and he was suspended.

Canadiens GM Kent Hughes said, "As soon as we saw that he asked to be traded we made the call." He added that he asked people to keep the plan quiet because he felt it would have been a distraction.

Larkin had 649 points (278 goals, 371 assists) in 818 regular-season games with Detroit. He has five seasons left on an eight-year, $69.6 million contract. He joins a 14-6-4 Montreal team coming off a trip to the Eastern Conference Final last season.

Captain Nick Suzuki said the added experience helps. "The more leaders and guys that have experience the better," he said.

Phillip Danault, who has faced Larkin for a decade, pointed to his "compete level, his speed, his way to make the difference."
