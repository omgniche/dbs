Fernando Mendoza will make his first NFL start Sunday as the Raiders face the defending Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks in Week 10.

The No. 1 pick in the 2026 draft and Heisman Trophy winner moves into the lineup after the Minnesota Vikings acquired Kirk Cousins at Tuesday's trade deadline. Las Vegas had signed Cousins before drafting Mendoza and planned to let the rookie develop behind him. After nine games, that plan has been shortened.

## A tough first test

Seattle owns the top-ranked defense in the league and is allowing the second-fewest passing yards of any team. Coach Klint Kubiak didn't sugarcoat it.

"Every week in the NFL is tough, and this is the best defense in the NFL," Kubiak said.

Mendoza said he has already watched plenty of Seahawks film. "They're a very impressive team," he said. "They're one of the best defenses in the league, so it's going to be a great challenge."

## Ready, according to the people around him

Kubiak said he is putting no limitations on the playbook for Mendoza's first start. The coach said the rookie has "exceeded those expectations" in practice, though he added that "live bullet Sunday is far different from practice."

Quarterbacks coach Mike Sullivan said Mendoza stays after practice for extra work, running through the script and his footwork. Mendoza got his first regular-season action Oct. 11 against the New England Patriots, finishing 6-for-8 for 48 yards late in the game.

Sullivan nicknamed his young passer "Spring Fernando," a nod to how far he has come since becoming a Raider. Edge rusher Malcolm Koonce, who lined up against him in OTAs, noticed a tendency to drift in the pocket. "I think now he honed it in and he does seem he can command the offense," Koonce said.

Tight end Brock Bowers and receiver Tre Tucker, soon-to-be top targets for the rookie, are excited too. Tucker predicted "fireworks." Mendoza, for his part, said he is focused on doing his "1/11th."
