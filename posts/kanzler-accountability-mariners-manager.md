Jason Kanzler addressed accountability at length during his introduction as the new Seattle Mariners manager last week, according to Daniel Kramer's Mariners Beat newsletter. He said it starts with making sure everyone knows exactly what they're responsible for.

"It requires clarity," Kanzler said. He added that clear expectations make hard conversations much easier.

This matters because accountability was the buzzword around the Mariners all through 2026. Players, coaches and the front office all brought it up, and much of the outside criticism said the team's self-inflicted mistakes weren't met with reprimand. Multiple clubhouse sources suggested the same, per the newsletter, and the result was more mistakes and more losses.

President of baseball operations Jerry Dipoto said accountability and communication were top criteria in the managerial search. Sitting next to Kanzler on Wednesday, he said the new skipper is clear in how he communicates.

"You won't be confused as to what the directive is when you leave the station, and I think our players will pick up on that," Dipoto said.

## How Kanzler plans to handle it

On tough conversations, Kanzler said, "Praise publicly, punish privately." He said there will be times to address the whole team, but that a manager has to pick his spots.

Kanzler is a first-time manager at any level and never played in the majors. Asked how he will challenge players, he said, "I ask a lot of questions." He said he listens and offers different ways of looking at a problem instead of simply telling players what to do.

Dipoto said after Dan Wilson's ouster that the team's "culture needs a re-set." Kanzler gave his own definition: "Culture is, in my opinion, it's all the things that you don't allow." He added, "What we allow is what we stand for, and so that's what culture means to me."
