Connor McDavid is NHL.com's unanimous choice to win the Hart Trophy at the first quarter of the 2026-27 season, the site announced in its Trophy Tracker series. He would be taking the award for the fourth time.

For Oilers fans, it's a look at what might be McDavid's best season yet. He has 55 points (13 goals, 42 assists) through 24 games, a 192-point pace. Only Wayne Gretzky has ever reached 200 points in a season.

McDavid's pace was at 200 until Thursday night, when he was held without a point for the first time this season in a 3-1 loss to the Tampa Bay Lightning. NHL.com says he would need a five-point game against the Carolina Hurricanes on Saturday to get back to that mark.

## The records piling up

NHL.com lists a long run of milestones so far:

- McDavid reached 50 points in Edmonton's 21st game, the fastest since Mario Lemieux did it in 18 games in 1995-96.
- He tied Gretzky as the fastest to 40 assists in a season, doing it in 22 games.
- He got to 30 assists in his 15th game, beating Gretzky's mark of 16 games from 1984-85.
- His 18-game assist streak to open the season is an NHL record. The old mark was 12.
- His 23-game point streak to start the year is the longest of his career and tied with Gretzky's 1988-89 run for fourth longest season-opening streak in league history.

McDavid talked about the team's new look earlier this month. "It's been really fun to just dive in and just have to kind of sink your teeth into a new system, a new way of playing, and it's kind of left my own game secondary," he said. Edmonton is playing under first-year coach Mike Babcock.

"We've all got to adhere to that structure and then you find your game within that game," Babcock said, "and he's been spectacular at that."

McDavid also credited his teammates. "When the team is winning games, individual stuff happens like that," he said, "and I've definitely been the benefactor of some great team play." Leon Draisaitl summed it up earlier this season: "It's just not real."

## How the voting shook out

McDavid got 75 points, including 15 first-place votes. Minnesota's Kirill Kaprizov was second with 50 points and Colorado's Nathan MacKinnon third with 38. Draisaitl placed fourth with 16 points, which gives Edmonton two players in the top four.
