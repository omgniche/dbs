The Mets have agreed to a deal with first baseman Paul Goldschmidt, a source told MLB.com's Anthony DiComo on Monday. The team has not confirmed the move.

It comes one day after the Mets landed free agent pitcher Tarik Skubal, so the spending is continuing.

First base was a problem last year. Jorge Polanco signed to fill the spot after Pete Alonso left, but injuries held him to 37 games and a .148 average. Jared Young and Mark Vientos ended up getting most of the time there.

Goldschmidt is 39 and arrives with a big resume: the 2022 MVP Award, seven All-Star selections, five Silver Slugger Awards and four Gold Gloves. He spent eight seasons with the D-backs and six with the Cardinals before joining the Yankees.

His best years are likely behind him, but he is still an above-average bat, especially against lefties. With the Yankees he posted a .765 OPS, 18 homers and a 111 OPS+ in 380 plate appearances. Against left-handers he hit .299/.393/.573 with 11 homers in 183 plate appearances. He has never finished a season below league average in OPS+.

There is a caution flag. His hard-hit rate was 35.9%, his lowest since Statcast began tracking in 2015.

## What else he brings

Goldschmidt still moves well at first, finishing with +6 Outs Above Average, per Statcast. He has also been durable and has not been on the injured list since 2014.

Over 16 seasons he owns an .876 OPS, 390 home runs and 65.1 bWAR. In 33 career playoff games, he has hit .262/.347/.533 with eight homers.
