The Mets have agreed to an eight-year, $95 million extension with outfielder A.J. Ewing, according to a source. The deal also includes a ninth-year team option for 2035 worth $25 million. The club has not confirmed the report.

For Mets fans, this is a big signal. It is the largest pre-arbitration contract in franchise history, topping the six-year, $55 million deal David Wright signed in 2006. It also locks a young center fielder into the long-term picture. Ewing is now under team control longer than anyone on the roster except Juan Soto.

Ewing, 22, debuted in May and started well before sliding into a deep slump over the summer. He bounced back with an .879 OPS over his final 25 games, which encouraged many Mets officials. According to one of them, he also expressed a sincere desire to live and play long-term in New York.

He finished his rookie year at .252/.324/.376 with 11 home runs and a club rookie record 25 stolen bases. He also showed he can handle center field and fill in at the corners. Ewing placed ninth in National League Rookie of the Year voting, three years after the Mets took him in the fourth round of the Draft.

One team official said "all the arrows pointed in the right direction" for a deal and called Ewing "the type of person you want to invest in."

## What it means for the Mets

The Mets have rarely gone this route with players who haven't reached arbitration. The exceptions were Wright and José Reyes in the mid-2000s, and Jon Niese and Juan Lagares the following decade. It's unclear whether they will try the same with Carson Benge, Nolan McLean or Christian Scott.

The deal averages $11.9 million a year. For Ewing, it guarantees a big payday no matter what happens. The tradeoff is that he gives up a shot at free agency in his 20s, though he could still hit the market at 30 or 31, depending on the 2035 option. The risk for the Mets is that the contract becomes an overpay if Ewing does not at least maintain his status as a solid everyday player, but team officials are confident he will not flop.
