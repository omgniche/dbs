The Mets pitched Tarik Skubal in person on Monday night in Orange County, Calif., and the free-agent lefty is set to meet with other suitors this week, according to MLB Network's Jon Heyman. Heyman said the meeting with New York went "very well."

The timing matters because Heyman reports the expectation is that Skubal will sign before the current Collective Bargaining Agreement expires on Dec. 1 at 11:59 p.m. ET. It isn't known whether the Mets have made an offer yet.

## Who else is in the mix

Heyman says at least six teams are scheduled to meet with Skubal. Besides the Mets, the known names are the Padres, Blue Jays and Dodgers, plus two clubs that haven't been identified. The dates for those meetings are not known.

MLB.com's Mark Feinsand wrote after last week's General Managers Meetings that "nearly everybody in the game expects Skubal to sign with the Mets." The reasoning is that New York needs starting pitching and few teams can match its willingness to spend. The Mets signed Juan Soto to a 15-year, $765 million deal two years ago.

Mets president of baseball operations David Stearns has pushed back on the idea that he avoids long-term deals for pitchers. "Every situation is unique, and it's been also well publicized even since I've been here -- we've engaged in extremely long-term negotiations for pitchers and we will do so again," Stearns said at the GM Meetings. The richest pitcher contract during his tenure is Sean Manaea's three-year, $75 million deal.

## The other suitors

Dodgers GM Brandon Gomes confirmed the club wants a reunion with Skubal, whom L.A. got from the Tigers last summer for three prospects. The Athletic's Katie Woo reported on Nov. 11 that their interest depends on whether he will take a shorter deal than other clubs offer. Kyle Tucker's four-year, $240 million contract with two opt-outs could be the model.

The Padres have more financial flexibility under new ownership, but they need to rebuild nearly their whole rotation. Toronto is doing its homework, but Skubal looks like an unlikely fit given the team's other needs.

The Cubs, Yankees, Phillies and Giants have all been mentioned. So far, nothing suggests any of them have meetings scheduled.
