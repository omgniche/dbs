Tarik Skubal sat down with Mets officials in California on Monday, MLB Network insider Jon Heyman reported via the New York Post. Heyman said the meeting "went very well, though it's hard to judge the significance of that." No concrete offers have been reported so far.

New York has been reported to be one of six teams in the mix for the lefty, along with the Dodgers, Padres, Blue Jays and two unnamed clubs. Dodgers general manager Brandon Gomes confirmed his team was set to meet with Skubal too. Skubal is reportedly expected to sign by Dec. 1.

## Why the Mets make sense

President of baseball operations David Stearns has said "there's no question" the Mets will add to a young rotation that includes Nolan McLean, Christian Scott, Zac Thornton and Jonah Tong. The club has also shown it will spend big, as its 15-year, $765 million agreement with Juan Soto in December 2024 showed.

Stearns has not yet given a free-agent pitcher more than the three years and $75 million Sean Manaea got that same offseason. There are no indications to suggest he and owner Steve Cohen would not stretch for Skubal. "Steve has demonstrated repeatedly throughout his ownership tenure that he's going to support baseball operations and baseball operations needs, and I expect that to be the case this offseason as well," Stearns said.

## What Skubal brings

Skubal, a two-time Cy Young Award winner, will pitch at age 30 next season. He posted a 2.72 ERA in 26 starts, split between the Tigers and Dodgers after a midseason trade to Los Angeles. He missed 44 days after surgery to remove loose bodies from his throwing elbow. In the postseason, he allowed just three runs over two starts.

He could set records for both total guarantee and average annual value for a pitcher. Yoshinobu Yamamoto holds the guarantee mark at 12 years and $325 million. Justin Verlander and Max Scherzer share the AAV record at $43.33 million.
