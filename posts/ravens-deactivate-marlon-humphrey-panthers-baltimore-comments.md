Ravens cornerback Marlon Humphrey will not play Sunday against the Carolina Panthers, and he has been sent home from the team facility. The move follows derogatory comments he made about the city of Baltimore on his podcast, "The Marlon Humphrey Show."

Head coach Jesse Minter told reporters Thursday that the decision came after "a lot of internal conversations" with the organization's leadership. He said those talks are personal and private and need to stay that way for the good of the team.

This matters because Humphrey is the longest-tenured member of Baltimore's defense. He is a 10-year veteran who has spent his whole career with the Ravens, with four Pro Bowl nods and two first-team All-Pro selections, in 2019 and 2024. The Ravens are 4-6 and going through one of the worst stretches in franchise history.

## What Humphrey said

On the podcast, Humphrey said Baltimore was "best at making criminals." The comments upset residents and fans. He apologized Wednesday, saying he knows "the effect that words have." He also pointed out that he has been in Baltimore his whole career, welcomed a son there and is an investor in the city.

Baltimore mayor Brandon Scott answered in a statement on social media. "A verbal apology is not enough," he wrote. Scott invited Humphrey to sit down with him and some Baltimoreans who have returned to the community and now work to keep others from making the mistakes they made.

Minter described Humphrey as "very remorseful." The coach said he wants to keep Humphrey out of the spotlight and cut down on distractions as the team tries to turn its season around. "This is a team thing, in my opinion," Minter said.
