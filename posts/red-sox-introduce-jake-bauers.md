The Red Sox officially introduced Jake Bauers at a Fenway Park press conference on Tuesday, days after the free-agent hitter agreed to a four-year, $58 million contract.

Bauers, 30, just had a career-best season with the Brewers, who went on to win the World Series. He was also the NLCS MVP. Boston president of baseball operations Craig Breslow says the team had its eye on him well before the playoffs.

"I told Jake he was the first call when free agency opened," Breslow said. He added that Bauers was the only player the team held a Zoom call with, because the fit seemed so strong.

## Why it matters

The Red Sox were knocked out by the Yankees in the Wild Card round the last two seasons. They see Bauers as the extra bat who can help them go deeper.

Breslow called him a middle-of-the-order hitter with "great plate discipline" who will get on base and walk, and has "enough power to hit the ball out of any park." He also said Bauers suits Fenway because of his ability to hit the ball in the air to left field. "We expect a huge season and a lot of doubles incoming," Breslow said.

Breslow said that Bauers changed his setup, bat path and approach, and that those changes convinced him 2026 was no fluke. He pointed to Bauers' low chase rate and hard contact over the years as proof the skills were already there. "This is who he's going to be for the next four years," Breslow said.

## How he fits

Barring injuries, Bauers fits best as the primary DH. He would also play some first base to give Willson Contreras a break, and could make occasional starts in left and right field.

Bauers said the turnaround came from a lot of mechanical and physical work, but mostly from a change in mindset. "I really bought into a team mentality, and I'm just here to do whatever I can to help the team win," he said.

He also said Breslow's Zoom pitch stuck with him and his wife. "It shot Boston up to the top of the list," Bauers said.
