Seahawks guard Quenton Nelson will have a minor knee procedure and is considered week-to-week, head coach Mike Macdonald announced Friday. Nelson, who Seattle acquired in a trade with the Colts last week, will miss Sunday's game against the Cardinals.

The news is a surprise, and it lands on top of a long injury list. Seattle will play without several starters this week, so the offensive line shuffle is only one piece of what the team has to sort out.

Macdonald said Nelson will not go on injured reserve. "There's a timeline right now that warrants not IR, so that's what we're going to do," he said.

According to Macdonald, Nelson was dealing with a knee issue when he arrived, and it was aggravated after he joined the team. Nelson was on last week's injury report as a full participant. "We've done the due diligence, and everybody agrees it's the best to take care of it now and play the long game," Macdonald said.

## Line changes

Grey Zabel had moved to right guard so Nelson could play his usual spot. Now Zabel goes back to left guard, where he played for his first season and a half in the league. Anthony Bradford, Seattle's starting right guard for most of his four-year career, returns to the starting lineup.

Macdonald praised how Bradford has handled the situation. "Anthony, he really deserves a tip of the cap for how he's handled the whole process," he said.

## Who else is out

Quarterback Sam Darnold, who hurt his quadricep in Monday night's game, was already ruled out. Also out are left tackle Charles Cross (ankle), defensive tackle Leonard Williams (stinger), outside linebacker Uchenna Nwosu (ankle), receiver Rashid Shaheed (knee) and nose tackle Brandon Pili (back). Outside linebacker Connor O'Toole is questionable with a foot injury.

Drew Lock will start at quarterback despite an elbow injury that kept him limited in practice all week. Macdonald said Lock's reduced workload has been "more precautionary than anything else." Jalen Milroe has been getting extra reps in case he is needed.

Macdonald said the depth is part of the plan. "This is a 70-man roster week, this is a strength of the Seahawks," he said.
