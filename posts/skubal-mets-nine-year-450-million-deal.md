Tarik Skubal is headed to the Mets. The two-time Cy Young Award-winning left-hander and the club have agreed to a nine-year, $450 million contract, a source told MLB.com's Mark Feinsand on Sunday afternoon. The Mets have not confirmed the deal.

For Mets fans, this is the biggest name on the free-agent market, and he is coming off a dominant stretch. Once the deal is finalized, it would be the richest contract ever signed by a pitcher, topping the $325 million Yoshinobu Yamamoto got from the Dodgers on a 12-year deal before the 2024 season.

Skubal turned 30 on Friday. From 2023 to 2026 he posted a 2.47 ERA, a 169 ERA+, a 2.42 FIP and a 6.94 strikeout-to-walk ratio, according to MLB.com. He became the 12th pitcher to win the Cy Young in back-to-back seasons when he took the American League award in 2024 and 2025.

In 2024 he won the AL pitching Triple Crown with 18 wins, a 2.39 ERA and 228 strikeouts, and he was a unanimous Cy Young choice. In 2025 he led the AL in ERA again at 2.21 and struck out 241 batters in 195 1/3 innings.

## A bumpy 2026 that ended strong

Skubal had surgery in May to remove loose bodies from his left elbow. He chose a less invasive NanoScope procedure, which shortened his recovery, and he returned to the Tigers' rotation on June 13, about six weeks after the operation.

Detroit was on the fringes of the playoff race and Skubal was headed for free agency, so the Tigers traded him to the Dodgers for three prospects on Aug. 2. Over his last 16 starts, 10 of them with Los Angeles, he had a 2.36 ERA and a 6.32 strikeout-to-walk ratio. That run eased the elbow concerns before he hit the market.

The Tigers drafted Skubal out of Seattle University in the ninth round in 2018. He had missed all of 2017 after Tommy John surgery. He had flexor tendon surgery in August 2022 and returned the following July, then took off from there.
