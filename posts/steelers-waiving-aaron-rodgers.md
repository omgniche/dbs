The Pittsburgh Steelers are waiving quarterback Aaron Rodgers, NFL Network's Ian Rapoport and ESPN's Adam Schefter reported Wednesday. The move follows Rodgers' appearance that day on "The Pat McAfee Show," where he made several allegations about head coach Mike McCarthy. Gerry Dulac of the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette reported it first.

This is a sharp turn for a team that, according to Rapoport, planned to keep Rodgers before the interview aired. Pittsburgh changed course afterward.

The drama started late Tuesday, when the team announced Rodgers would be inactive for Friday's game against the Broncos and that Mason Rudolph would start. That came just hours after McCarthy had publicly said Rodgers would remain the starter following a poor offensive showing in a loss to Philadelphia.

## What Rodgers said

On the show, Rodgers said he found out about the benching from tight end Pat Freiermuth. He also said it came after he spoke up to McCarthy about the coach falling asleep and snoring in position meetings or missing team events. Rodgers said he also talked with McCarthy about changing play-calling duties.

Rodgers said: "If you try and bench me or don’t change the play calling then you should just release me, because I feel like at this point, I’m doing what’s right for the organization."

He said McCarthy responded that he would do what's right by the organization, which, in McCarthy's mind according to Rodgers, was getting rid of him. "Now obviously he got the owner and the GM on his side," Rodgers said.

Rodgers also said he broke the huddle at halftime of the Week 11 loss to the Eagles by saying, "1-2-3 Packers."

These are Rodgers' own claims, and the source report doesn't include a response from McCarthy. NFL.com has reached out to the Steelers for comment. The Associated Press contributed to the report.
