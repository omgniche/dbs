The Minnesota Wild could hold on to Quinn Hughes beyond the March 1 trade deadline even if he hasn't signed a new contract, general manager Bill Guerin said Tuesday, according to NHL.com. Asked whether he'd be willing to do that, Guerin answered, "Sure," and then, "Yeah."

Guerin described where things stand with Hughes as "status quo." He said the reason is that he wants the defenseman to focus on hockey.

Hughes, 27, is in the final season of a six-year, $47.1 million deal ($7.85 million average annual value) that he signed with the Vancouver Canucks on Oct. 3, 2021. Minnesota acquired him in a trade on Dec. 12, 2025. He has been eligible to sign a new deal with the Wild since July 1. If he doesn't sign by June 30, he becomes an unrestricted free agent the next day.

On Oct. 24, Hughes said he wouldn't be surprised if something happened soon. Guerin was asked when he might hit an inflection point and said he didn't know, adding, "Maybe I don't."

Guerin was clear about how much Hughes means to the team. "Honestly, we're a better team with Quinn Hughes, so I mean, I want to keep him as long as I possibly can," he said.

Hughes, who won the Norris Trophy in 2024, has 79 points (11 goals, 68 assists) in 67 games since the trade. That's second on the Wild and second among NHL defensemen in that span. Only Edmonton's Evan Bouchard has more among defensemen, with 98 points in 70 games.

The Wild are 12-3-4 and lead the Central Division. They were set to play at the Montreal Canadiens on Tuesday.
